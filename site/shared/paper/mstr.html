How the composite z is built

The composite z-score is the equal-weight mean of the five speeds' clipped z-scores, then re-standardised over a trailing 120-day window and clipped to ±3. Each speed's z comes from its own medcouple vs its trailing history; the medcouple percentile is causal / expanding (ranked against that speed's prior observations only).

MSTR/IBIT relative-value paper trade — summary

The signal is a 5-speed equal-weight medcouple ensemble (trailing windows 10/15/20/30/45 days, 200 IBIT-clocked dollar bars) of MSTR/IBIT dollar-bar VWAP log-returns, z-scored and clipped, sized $10k per unit of signal, dollar-neutral. It began as an execution-plumbing exercise on an IBKR paper account, not a profit expectation (exploratory correlation ~0.03).

It is currently paper-only and not funded: a cost-sensitivity study found no tradeable net-of-cost edge (details below), so the hourly runner is suspended. The live-signal panel above is kept as a monitoring/research view — it is computed offline from stored bars and places no orders.

What was learned

  • Direction is thin. The medcouple ensemble's directional edge is small (IC ~0.02–0.04) and concentrated in low-volatility hours; it does not survive realistic transaction costs at any cadence or filter.
  • Costs dominate. SMART routing cannot net the MSTR/IBIT pair (two unrelated underlyings, no listed combo), so both single-name half-spreads (~7.5 bps MSTR, ~3.2 bps IBIT per leg) are paid on every rebalance. That double spread is the binding constraint, not latency or signal freshness.
  • Volatility is forecastable and useful for sizing. The 10-day-speed per-minute vol predicts the forward absolute move (R²~0.07), and inverse-vol sizing roughly doubles gross Sharpe (0.30→0.81) — but as risk-normalisation, not new directional alpha.
  • Best gross configuration (low-vol-gated, 10-min, hold-through-deadzone + hysteresis) reaches a gross Sharpe ~2.25, yet still loses money net after the double spread. The remaining obstacle is turnover cost, not signal.

Why it was retired

A cost-sensitivity study over 2026-01-01 → 2026-10-09, using live-measured half-spreads (MSTR ~7.5 bps, IBIT ~3.2 bps per leg), found the gross edge too thin to clear transaction costs at any cadence or filter. SMART routing cannot net the pair (MSTR and IBIT are unrelated underlyings with no listed combo), so both single-name half-spreads are paid on every rebalance.

Every realistic configuration is net-negative. Faster trading raises gross alpha but spread cost rises faster; slower trading loses the edge; conviction filters and no-trade bands help only marginally. The one non-negative point (+$204) is small-sample noise from a non-monotonic, un-pinnable threshold. Execution latency was shown not to be the constraint.
