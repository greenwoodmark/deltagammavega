TIP vs TLT model: detailed research note

This note explains the research-only experiment that combines an option-implied distribution with historical physical-return information. The purpose is to test whether a constrained entropy update improves one-month ETF return forecasts. It is not a production deployment recommendation.

1. The Q prior

Let the option-implied model provide a discrete distribution over normalized terminal prices:

\[ U_i = \frac{P_{T,i}}{F}, \qquad q_i > 0, \qquad \sum_i q_i=1. \]

Here, F is the option-implied forward. The distribution is centered so that:

\[ E_Q[U] = \sum_i q_i U_i = 1. \]

For spot-based log returns, the support is:

\[ X_i = \log\left(\frac{F}{S_0}\right)+\log(U_i). \]

The Q distribution is therefore the market-implied prior for the next one-month terminal return. It is forward-looking and is not assumed to equal the historical physical distribution.

2. Physical information

Using an as-of-filtered hourly ETF price history, the research estimator forms overlapping one-calendar-month returns. It estimates two robust features:

  • Scale: IQR divided by 1.349.
  • Skew: primarily Bowley skewness, with medcouple retained as a benchmark.

The uncertainty of these estimates is measured with a circular moving-block bootstrap of hourly returns. The result is a target vector and covariance matrix:

\[ \hat{s}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{s}_{\mathrm{scale}}\\\hat{s}_{\mathrm{skew}}\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \Sigma=\operatorname{Cov}(\hat{s}). \]

The block bootstrap accounts for dependence in the hourly data, but it remains an estimate. Its covariance determines how strongly the physical information is trusted.

3. Forward-constrained entropy update

The posterior probabilities p remain on the same Q support. They are chosen by minimizing:

\[ \min_{p}\;D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q) +\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(s(P)-\hat{s}\right)^\top \Sigma^{-1}\left(s(P)-\hat{s}\right), \]

subject to:

\[ \sum_i p_i=1, \qquad p_i\geq 0, \qquad \sum_i p_i U_i=1. \]

The first term penalizes movement away from Q. The second term rewards agreement with the historical physical views, adjusted for their estimated uncertainty. The parameter alpha controls view strength: alpha=0 returns Q, while alpha=1 is the original full update.

Computationally, the posterior is an exponential tilt of Q:

\[ p_i \propto q_i\exp\left(\lambda_s f_{s,i}+\lambda_k f_{k,i}+\lambda_F U_i\right). \]

For every pair of shape multipliers, the forward multiplier \(\lambda_F\) is solved so that \(E_P[U]=1\) exactly. Thus the update can alter dispersion and skew, but cannot change the normalized arithmetic forward.

4. How efficacy was tested

The posterior and Q prior were evaluated on realized one-month TLT returns over five quarterly periods, using 298 successful scored observations. CRPS measures overall distributional distance, with lower values better. A Gaussian-kernel log score measures density assigned near the realized return, with higher values better. Central coverage and interval width were recorded as diagnostics, not as the primary objective.

Aggregate Q results were log score 1.827, CRPS 0.02137, central-90% coverage 89.6%, and mean width 0.1357. The full Bowley posterior increased coverage to 96.6%, but its log score fell to -19.36, CRPS rose to 0.09757, and width expanded to 0.5768.

A targeted alpha-0.05 test found that medcouple was less damaging than Bowley but still did not consistently beat Q. A view ablation showed that scale-only was approximately neutral and marginally improved scores, whereas skew-only produced most of the widening and deterioration. The current conclusion is therefore to retain Q as the forecasting baseline and keep the entropy update research-only.

5. Simple analogy: estimating a biased coin

Suppose Q assigns probability \(q\) to heads. For example, let:

\[ q=0.50. \]

After observing 70 heads in 100 independent flips, a physical estimate might be:

\[ \hat{p}=0.70. \]

Rather than replace Q mechanically, combine it with the uncertain observation through:

\[ \min_{0\leq p\leq 1} \left[p\log\frac{p}{q}+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}\right] +\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{(p-\hat{p})^2}{\sigma^2}. \]

The first bracket is the Bernoulli KL divergence from Q. The second term measures disagreement with the observed physical estimate, scaled by its uncertainty \(\sigma\).

  • If the 70% estimate is precise, for example \(\sigma=0.05\), the posterior moves materially above 50%.
  • If it is uncertain, for example \(\sigma=0.20\), the posterior remains closer to 50%.
  • If \(\alpha=0\), the posterior is exactly Q: 50%.
  • For any positive alpha, the posterior normally lies between Q and the physical estimate, unless a boundary or additional constraint intervenes.

The ETF problem is the same idea in a richer setting. Q supplies a forward-looking distribution rather than a single probability, while historical returns supply uncertain scale and skew information. The important distinction is that one realized month is only one coin flip: it cannot by itself identify a full future distribution. Out-of-sample scoring is therefore necessary before trusting the update.

6. Research decision

Current decision: use the option-implied Q distribution as the forecasting baseline. Do not deploy the current entropy-updated posterior. Future research should test recency weighting, regime conditioning, alternative tail views, and explicit covariance calibration.
