TIP vs TLT options relative value

This project studies relative value between TIP, the iShares TIPS Bond ETF, and TLT, the iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF. It combines daily one-month GS Marquee volatility smiles with GH5 distribution fits and high-frequency ETF returns. The research goal is to model joint TIP/TLT behaviour, including Student-t copula dependence across horizons, then evaluate long-TIP/short-TLT 10-delta put and call trades under explicit risk-neutral and real-world assumptions. Future Development Paths

Research question: how can we identify the portfolio containing one TLT put or call option and one TIP put or call option, each at feasible strikes—initially between approximately 5-delta and 50-delta—that offers the best expected future return on a risk-adjusted basis?

Risk will initially be measured as the standard deviation of the portfolio's one-month log returns. We will simulate the joint density of one-month TLT and TIP log returns to evaluate candidate option combinations and identify the portfolio with the most attractive expected-return-to-risk trade-off.

The initial experiment will assume:

  • zero initial delta for the portfolio;
  • no intermediate hedging;
  • interest and dividends ignored;
  • dealing at mid-market price levels implied by the GH model; and
  • one TLT option and one TIP option, with each strike restricted to the feasible delta range.

These assumptions define a transparent first experiment. They can later be relaxed to include non-zero initial delta, dynamic hedging, financing and carry, dividends, bid-offer spreads, transaction costs, and alternative execution prices.

Starting distributional approach: the research to date shows that improving on the one-month risk-neutral implied density as an estimator of subsequent ETF returns is difficult. We will therefore initially resample the marginal TLT and TIP distributions from their GH5 implied densities under the Q-measure, rather than using the entropy-updated posterior. We will concentrate first on a t-copula to model dependence between the two marginal distributions and simulate joint portfolio outcomes.

For each feasible pair of option strikes and option types, the simulation will calculate the option payoff, initial mid-market premium, terminal portfolio value, one-month return, expected return, standard deviation of log returns, and risk-adjusted performance measure. The resulting grid will show whether the preferred trade is driven by marginal volatility/skew, cross-ETF dependence, option convexity, or the relative pricing of the two smiles.

Research Notes

Entropy-updated posterior versus the Q prior

Across five quarterly TLT samples from 2024Q1 to 2025Q1, the entropy-updated posterior generally did not improve one-month return forecasting relative to the option-implied Q-measure prior. Q achieved an aggregate Gaussian-kernel log score of 1.827 and CRPS of 0.02137, with 89.6% central-90% coverage and mean interval width of 0.1357. The full Bowley update substantially widened the posterior: coverage rose to 96.6%, but log score deteriorated to -19.36, CRPS increased to 0.09757, and mean width expanded to 0.5768. At alpha=0.05, the more robust medcouple reduced the damage, producing log score about 1.77, CRPS 0.02139, and width 0.1379, but still did not beat Q consistently. A view ablation found that IQR scale-only was essentially benign and marginally improved scores, while skew-only caused most of the widening and deterioration. The evidence points primarily to the skew adjustment and update geometry, rather than bootstrap width alone. The Q prior should remain the forecasting baseline; entropy updates remain research-only and are not recommended for deployment.

Common-shape GH5 terminal backtest

This latest research iteration retained the market-implied GH5 marginal distributions for TIP and TLT but imposed a common higher-moment shape. Each ETF kept its own (b) parameter, forward, and scale characteristics, while (g), (h), (c), and (q) were replaced by their cross-ETF averages. The resulting pooled-shape distributions were used only for terminal scenario generation; original GH5-based market premiums, implied volatilities, and inception deltas were retained. Dependence was estimated from overlapping one-month TIP/TLT returns using exponentially weighted Spearman correlation, converted to a Gaussian copula. A 100×100 scenario grid evaluated portfolios containing 100 long units of a TIP 25-delta put or call against a short TLT option, with TLT delta searched from 5% to 50% and notional chosen to impose zero delta-volatility exposure. Both put and call strategies produced negative simulated Sharpe ratios and negative realised P&L. The optimizer usually selected the 5-delta TLT option, suggesting no opportunity under this common-shape hypothesis.

Historical GH5 ensemble and PIT recalibration

A possible alternative to the entropy-updated physical marginal is to use the historical GH5 fits themselves as an exponentially weighted ensemble. The motivation is practical: at each forecast date there is only one newly realised one-month TIP return and one newly realised one-month TLT return, while the entropy update has not consistently improved forecasting. Historical GH5 fits provide a longer record of the market-implied distributional shapes without assuming that a single physical update is reliable.

For every matured historical fit, record the realised one-month log return for each ETF and invert that date's GH5 marginal to obtain its probability-integral-transform (PIT) percentile. The realised return is therefore represented by its position within the distribution that was available at the time. PIT values can be analysed separately for TIP and TLT, and jointly through their dependence structure, to identify systematic under- or over-dispersion, skew, and tail-calibration errors.

For the current date, evaluate each historical GH5 fit at a common grid of standard-normal driver percentiles. Convert each historical normalized terminal-price distribution to the current forward, rather than averaging absolute prices. In log-return terms, the aligned historical outcome at percentile p is log(F_current / S_current) + log(U_h(p)), where U_h(p) is the historical normalized terminal-price quantile. This removes changes in the historical forward level while retaining the fitted distributional shape.

Exponentially average the aligned historical quantiles using the fixed three-month half-life, with alpha = log(2) / 91.25, to produce an ensemble marginal. This should be compared walk-forward against the current GH5 Q marginal and a PIT-recalibrated current GH5 marginal, constructed by mapping current Q percentiles through the historical weighted PIT distribution. The ensemble is not an independent expansion of the realised sample: overlapping observations remain dependent. It is a structured forecast combining historical implied-shape information with observed calibration errors, and must be evaluated strictly out of sample without using the current month's realised percentile.

Implication for copula-based portfolio research

If both TIP and TLT marginals remain the latest GH5 option-implied distributions under the risk-neutral Q-measure, then changing the copula family or its admissible dependence parameter changes the joint covariance and tail risk, but not the expected payoff of either standalone vanilla option. Consequently, an additive long-one-put/short-one-put portfolio has zero expected Q-measure P&L when each premium is calculated from its corresponding marginal. Its simulated Sharpe ratio is therefore zero, apart from numerical or discretisation error, regardless of the copula or Spearman correlation.

A positive expected Sharpe requires a distinct physical P-measure forecast whose marginal option payoffs differ from their Q-priced premiums; changing only the copula while leaving both marginals at Q cannot create expected return. The copula remains valuable for comparing portfolio variance, covariance, downside quantiles, and tail losses, and for pricing genuinely joint claims, but it cannot by itself generate alpha in this additive portfolio. The backtest should therefore separate marginal risk-premium assumptions from copula-risk analysis and avoid selecting a portfolio by maximising a numerically generated Q-Sharpe ratio.
