Jupiter / JUP EPS model

The performance-fee EPS model estimates Jupiter’s fee contribution by applying documented fund fee rates to positive returns above each fund’s benchmark and high-water-mark hurdle, multiplied by interpolated fund AUM. Inputs include annual-report AUM anchors, official NAV histories, KIID fee terms, benchmark assumptions, tax, variable compensation, overhead, and average shares outstanding.

For each observation window, the applicable annual rate is compounded over actual calendar days; only closing NAV above that hurdle generates fee-bearing excess return. Excess return is multiplied by interpolated average fund AUM and the assumed period average performance-fee rate: 20% in FY2024, 19% for FY2025, and 18% for FY2026. To address public UCITS coverage blind spots, the fee base is uplifted by assumed additional offshore Cayman and segregated-fund exposure of 20%, 30%, and 30% respectively, with performance assumed similar to the UCITS vehicle. Estimated PF PBT applies 45% staff/bonus drag and 2.5% overhead; estimated PF PAT applies 25% tax, then divides by period-average shares. FY2024 combines the archived FE fundinfo representative series through 27 November 2024 with official Jupiter NAV thereafter. FY2026 scenarios extend current NAV and AUM to year-end 2026.

D. Model assumptions

The performance-fee estimates are scenario calculations, not administrator or statutory accounting outputs. They use GEAR NAV before the performance-fee deduction but after ongoing fund expenses, and assume that movements into and out of units are ignored. The unit position is therefore treated as constant throughout each calculation period.

High-water markPermanent
A fee is charged only above the carried HWM. A loss or partial recovery does not lower it; after a positive annual crystallisation, the post-fee NAV becomes the next HWM.
CrystallisationAnnual
Daily provisional fees can reverse during drawdowns; the HWM transition occurs only at the accounting-period crystallisation date.
Unit movementsIgnored
The examples assume a constant unit balance and do not model subscriptions, redemptions, or investor-level flow adjustments.

Hurdle and fee-rate assumptions. The ESMA-style absolute-return test cases use a zero hurdle. The historical backtest uses annual hurdle assumptions of 5.3% for FY2024, 4.4% for FY2025, and 4.0% for FY2026, with average performance-fee rates of 20%, 19%, and 18% respectively. The FY2026 scenarios use a 4% hurdle and an 18% average performance-fee rate.

Corporate EPS bridge. Estimated gross performance fees are based on interpolated average AUM and assumed additional non-UCITS fee-bearing exposure of 20% in FY2024 and 30% in FY2025/FY2026. The bridge applies 45% staff/bonus drag, 2.5% overhead, and 25% tax before dividing by the relevant period-average share count. The FY2026 scenarios use £7.825bn interpolated average AUM and 526.8m shares. The FY2024/FY2025 comparison also uses the 2.76% FY2025 calibration-yield proxy described above.

Data and interpretation. FY2024 combines FE fundinfo-derived representative NAV history through 27 November 2024 with official Jupiter NAV from 28 November 2024 onward. AUM values are model anchors/interpolations rather than a complete observed AUM history. These displayed tables are static scenario outputs and should not be interpreted as live forecasts or administrator accruals.

Test coverage: 13 dedicated HWM/performance-fee tests cover positive crystallisation, post-fee HWM carry-forward, losses, partial recovery, breakout above the HWM, daily accrual reversal, the four-period ESMA example, and period-estimator carry behaviour. The wider Jupiter valuation test suite currently contains 74 passing tests.

F. Delta of JUP gross total return to GEAR performance

Each point compares the trailing 90-calendar-day GEAR NAV growth with the trailing 90-calendar-day gross dividend-reinvested JUP total return. The point date is the GEAR valuation date; each series uses the latest available observation on or before the current and prior dates. JUP total return starts from the unadjusted IBKR closing price and attributes pre-tax cash dividends on their ex-dates. The fitted line is an ordinary-least-squares regression of JUP total return on GEAR growth; beta is its slope, measured in JUP percentage points per one percentage point of GEAR growth.

Regression-implied JUP response is loading from the latest GEAR/JUP chart data.
